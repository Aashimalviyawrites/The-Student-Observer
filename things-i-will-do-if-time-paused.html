Things I Will Do If Time Paused

I could start somewhere and end up somewhere else completely. But since when do subjects have boundaries?

It is not a new thing. It all began in primary classes. The NCERT books didn't completely separate subjects into boxes. A maths chapter could introduce me to varieties of fish I had never seen, as someone who doesn't eat fish. A Hindi chapter could teach me history, society, and things beyond the written words. Later, in secondary classes, we were given interdisciplinary projects combining different subjects. And then there is my own research, which rarely stays confined to the question I started with.

Perhaps that is why I am interested in almost everything. But if you ask me to pick a hobby for you, I can't say a word. One day, my mother asked me the same question. I like it when my mother, who is both my friend and teacher, asks for my advice or opinion, so I gave her an honest answer.

Don't ask me. Every time I suggested something, you never got into it. Pick something yourself. Something you can do even when you are tired. And yeah, something that, for God's sake, is not for self-improvement. Otherwise, you will leave it in a few days when the motivation runs out. Because I believe that the purpose of a hobby is to make you feel happy, not simply productive.

And the irony is that I have a thousand hobbies on my wishlist. Writing poetry, reading history, debating politics, coding, researching, learning about space, singing, observing society, trying something new and diving into something I once believed was "not my thing". I have found myself with two evening hours and a hundred unrelated things I want to learn. Most of them require years of practice and dedication to master.

Somehow, choosing one feels like a betrayal of another. So, when I feel overwhelmed by all the things I want to do, I open a specific notebook. I write: Things I will do if time paused.

And then, without thinking, I write down everything I have felt like doing over the past few days. At the end, I usually have around thirty or forty time-demanding, completely complicated, unrelated things written down. I choose one. Sit down. Do a bit of whatever I want to do. When it is done, I mark a tick.

Sometimes I return to something after a few weeks, randomly see that I have finally finished it, and mark that tick. Not that day. Not that month. But one day, all of them get finished. And then the cycle repeats.

Recently, my father saw me doing riyaz, something I had started about a month earlier. He was surprised. A week before that, he had seen me writing a history essay. Before that, I was enrolled in an ISRO course. Before that, I was coding. Before that, I was writing poetry. And now this.

"What do you actually want to be when you grow up?" he asked. I didn't have an answer. I still don't. But I told him a story. When Steve Jobs dropped out of college, he took a calligraphy class. It was something he found interesting, without knowing what it would eventually lead to. Years later, when Apple was developing the Macintosh, that experience became relevant to the typography of its computers. He didn't know, when he began, what he was doing it for.

That was my answer to my father. And I was proud of it.Why must we always know the outcome of everything we spend our time on, if it is something we find interesting?

I know that the things I do for fun today might not change my life one day. They might just remain hobbies, reminding me that it is not a waste of time if you love it.

Perhaps this is also why I find it so difficult to leave any interests behind. They do not exist in separate boxes. One leads me to another, which takes me further into something I hadn't intended to explore. And somewhere along the way, I find another thing I want to learn.

I know I cannot do everything. Time does not pause to let me finish those fifty things I want to learn. I will have to choose, leave things for later, and accept that some things may never get done. But I don't want to look at every unfinished interest as a failure. It may already have done what it was supposed to do: make me feel curious, engaged, and more alive.

I still don't know what I want to be when I grow up.

For now, I have a notebook full of things I will do if time paused.
